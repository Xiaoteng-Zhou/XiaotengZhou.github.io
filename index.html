01About

I am an underwater robotics researcher working at the intersection of acoustic sensing, physical modelling and intelligent perception. My work focuses on making underwater structures observable when optical methods fail: I develop sonar-based methods for detecting, tracking, mapping and reconstructing underwater scenes in turbid, confined and low-visibility water, and carry them from analytical models and Python-based algorithms through water-tank experiments, towing-tank trials and field deployments.

A central application is the intelligent inspection of marine assets and offshore energy infrastructure — quay walls, piers, foundations, moorings, pipelines and other subsea structures — where robot-borne sonar must deliver reliable geometry and condition information under real operating constraints. Related work covers marine-debris monitoring, plume tracking, benthic environmental investigation and ecological analysis. I completed my Ph.D. in Marine Acoustic Perception at The University of Tokyo and hold an M.E. in Ocean Engineering from Harbin Institute of Technology.

Capabilities for inspection solutions

  • Sonar-based structural inspection. High-resolution imaging-sonar mapping, adaptive mosaicking and rapid 2.5-D/3-D reconstruction of walls, seabed and structures in low-visibility and confined spaces.
  • Robot localisation and navigation. Sonar SLAM-style mapping, USBL fusion, acoustic-marker pose estimation and active viewpoint planning for ROV/AUV survey paths around structures.
  • Multi-sensor integration. Fusing sonar with optical, hyperspectral and navigation sensors into a coherent survey pipeline, from data acquisition and time synchronisation to georeferenced outputs.
  • Acoustic modelling and simulation. Physics-based models of propagation, reflection, backscatter, multipath and clutter, used for sensor selection, survey design and generating training data before field work.
  • AI-driven detection and monitoring. Deep-learning and feature-based methods for target detection, tracking and change analysis in sonar imagery, benchmarked against manual annotation.
  • Field validation. End-to-end trial design — water tank, towing tank and in-situ deployments with platforms such as BlueROV2 — with quantitative evaluation against ground truth.
